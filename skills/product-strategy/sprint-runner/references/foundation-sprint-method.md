# Foundation Sprint — Team Method (phase-gated)

From *Click* (Knapp & Zeratsky) and the Character Capital guide, adapted for a facilitated virtual-team run. Phases gate on artifacts + logged decisions, never on time. Book timings are omitted deliberately — an exercise ends when its round completes.

The sprint uses **only the team's existing knowledge** — no research phase. (Exception: verifying a checkable market claim someone asserted.) Its power is forcing implicit predictions into the open where the Decider can commit or kill them cheaply.

## F1 — Basics

Four exercises, each a full note-and-vote loop (SKILL.md engine). Whiteboard per exercise.

| Exercise | Note-round prompt (same to team and user) | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Target customer | "Who exactly is this for? Plain language, real people. Narrow beats broad — name a person you could actually go find." | Vague segments ("busy professionals"). Push for findable humans. |
| Customer problem | "What's the biggest pain we relieve for <decided customer>? Is it painful enough to justify the time/money/effort of switching?" | Problems that are inconveniences. If nobody's currently paying (time or money) to solve it, flag it. |
| Your advantage | "What's the user's unfair edge — capability (can do what others can't), insight (understands what others don't), motivation (will outlast others)?" The **user answers first** here — it's about them; teammates then probe and challenge rather than invent. | Advantage theater. At least one dimension must be honestly strong; the skeptic tests each claim. |
| Competitors | "Every option the customer has today — including workarounds and **do nothing**. Which is the 800-pound gorilla (hardest to beat)?" | If "do nothing" wins the vote, the problem may not be painful enough — say so out loud before the Decider call. |

Gate: 4 decisions in DECISIONS.md, F1 boxes ticked.

## F2 — Differentiation

The crux: make the gorilla look like junk *for this customer*.

1. **Classic differentiators** — post spectrums (fast, easy, cheap/free, simple, focused, premium, fun, trustworthy, flexible, beautiful — adapt to domain). Team + user dot-vote where this product could credibly beat the gorilla. Optimistic AND realistic.
2. **Custom differentiators** — note-and-vote criteria specific to the advantage/insight where this product shines and rivals look crummy.
3. **Pick two** — Decider chooses the two differentiators to lead with.
4. **Build the 2x2** — two differentiators as axes; plot the product + every competitor (portable notation in templates.md). Iterate the axis labels with the team until the product sits **alone top-right** and rivals fall into Loserville (the L of the other three quadrants). If it can't get there honestly, the differentiation isn't sharp — go back to step 2. The skeptic's explicit job here: argue the gorilla into the top-right.
5. **Principles** — note-and-vote 2–3 practical principles that deliver the differentiation ("fast" → "Fast is better than slow"). These guide the prototype later.

Gate: **Mini Manifesto** (two differentiators + principles, one page) in OUTPUTS.md.

## F3 — Approach (the pre-pivot)

1. **Options round** — note-and-vote distinct ways to deliver the value (product, service, tool, marketplace, content, concierge, AI-native…). Each option: name, one-line why-it's-good, 2–3 line how-it-works. Decider keeps up to 7, each labeled A–G.
2. **Magic Lenses** — one 2x2 per lens; plot ALL kept options on each. Four classics + 1–3 custom:

| Lens | Question | Default axes (low ←→ high) |
|---|---|---|
| Customer | Best solves the problem / most value? | weakly ←→ strongly solves · low ←→ high value |
| Pragmatic | Most feasible for us? | hard ←→ easy to build · slow ←→ fast to ship |
| Growth | Spreads and scales best? | hard ←→ easy to grow · weak ←→ strong distribution |
| Money | Best business potential? | low ←→ high revenue · weak ←→ strong margins |
| Custom | unique-to-us, founder excitement, defensibility, time-to-first-value, no-ads-required… | fit to lens |

   Team adaptation: assign each lens to the 1–2 personas whose bias fits (growth persona plots the Growth lens, etc.); they propose placements with one-line reasons; the user adjusts any placement; re-plot on the F2 differentiation 2x2 as a bonus lens.
3. **Zoom out** — does one option keep landing top-right? Do lenses contradict (a real tradeoff — name it, don't smooth it)?
4. **Decide** — one **Top Bet** + one **Backup Plan** (Decider).

Gate: Top Bet + Backup in DECISIONS.md, lens table in whiteboards/.

## F4 — Founding Hypothesis

1. Assemble the exact Mad-Libs template from the decisions (no new debate — every blank was already decided):

> **If we help [customer] solve [problem] with [approach], they will choose it over [competitors] because our solution is [differentiator 1] + [differentiator 2].**

2. **Attack it** — each teammate names the clause they trust least, with a one-line reason (this is a critique round, no votes).
3. **Prove-it scorecard** — for each prediction (right customer? right problem? right approach? will they really switch? do they care about these differentiators? does it click?): the cheapest test that could falsify it + risk H/M/L. Decider marks the **riskiest assumption**.
4. **First move** — the single cheapest experiment against the riskiest assumption (often: a Design Sprint — offer chained mode).

Gate: Hypothesis + scorecard + first move in OUTPUTS.md. Sprint complete.

## Technique notes

- **Note-and-Vote beats open brainstorming** because it removes anchoring and loudest-voice effects — which is exactly why the ordering invariants in SKILL.md exist. Don't let a teammate's early answer anchor the user: collect the user's notes in the same blind round.
- **Clarity, not consensus.** Ties and splits are information; the Decider breaks them, you never do.
- **Simple promises win.** At every convergence, prefer the answer that makes the eventual hypothesis sentence simpler.
